# M4 deck planking — revised gap analysis and proposed standard-lumber pattern

**Status: implemented 2026-11-11; this is the approved review basis.**
The live model now contains the standard-lumber deck pattern. See §0 for the
as-built results, validation and remaining items. The review text below is
retained; references to the “109-segment/53-run” deck describe the
pre-implementation state.

- **12 ft is a hard maximum board length** (standard 12-ft lumber); shorter
  boards are allowed.
- **Standard 4-in or 6-in nominal deck plank stock** (dressed widths 3½ in and
  5½ in).
- **Every internal butt terminates at the centreline of a measured deck beam**,
  so the two adjoining boards each cover half the beam’s sided width and share
  the beam. **No ledgers and no unsupported butts.**
- The working assumption that these were cheaply built boats and the deck was
  therefore built from standard lumber, consistent with the printed widths.

## 1. Summary

At review, the M4 deck was a **material partition**, not a plank schedule:

- 109 single-solid segments in 53 longitudinal runs (`parts/deck_planking.py`).
- Runs are parallel constant-width strips, split only where a hatch/cabin
  opening crosses them.
- There are no physical plank lengths, no staggered butt joints, and no
  beam-centred ends.
- At the bow and stern the strips are simply cut by the hull outline, leaving
  triangular tips rather than spiled/tapered plank ends.
- The deck boards currently include the material at the deck margin; the oak
  waterway is not a separate member.
- At review the typical board width was **3.75 in** and the centre run
  **6.0 in**. The implemented deck resolves them to **3½ in actual (4-in
  nominal)** and **5½ in actual (6-in nominal)**.

Sheet 5 (S05-V01) shows longitudinal deck planks whose seams converge and
terminate on the curved waterway at different fore-aft stations, and the
openings interrupt individual planks. This is the staggered/tapered pattern the
model does not yet represent. The plan does **not** print individual butt
positions or plank lengths, and the photos cannot resolve them. The standard
12-ft maximum, the 4-in/6-in stock widths and the beam-centred butt layout
below are therefore a **qualified reconstruction**, consistent with the
drawings and with cheap production practice.

## 4. Proposed deck planking specification

### 4.1 Goals / acceptance

1. Build the deck from **standard lumber**: 4-in or 6-in nominal plank widths,
   with no custom widths.
2. Every deck plank is a separate valid single solid with a logical board ID.
3. **No board is longer than 12 ft.** Shorter boards are allowed; the layout
   must be cuttable from standard 12-ft stock.
4. **Every internal butt is centred on a measured deck beam.** The upstream
   plank ends at the beam centreline and covers one half of the beam’s sided
   width; the downstream plank starts at the same centreline and covers the
   other half. The two boards share the beam. No ledgers and no unsupported
   butts are permitted.
5. Adjacent planks are staggered by at least two beam bays (approximately
   4–6 ft); no two adjacent planks share a butt station; no continuous
   transverse butt line crosses the deck.
6. Plank ends are spiled/tapered into the waterway; no triangular strip tips.
7. The total deck material still equals the accepted deck envelope
   (boards + waterway). No extra backing is introduced.
8. Openings, stairs, helm clearances and hatch access remain clear.
9. The actual export has all board meshes and no missing faces.

### 4.2 Parameters (all qualified)

| Parameter | Proposed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Deck plank thickness | 1.75 in actual | S05-08 (“1-3/4 inches”) |
| **Typical plank width** | **3½ in actual (4-in nominal)** | **Standard 4-in lumber; S05-08 says 3½–4 in** |
| **Inboard / kingplank width** | **5½ in actual (6-in nominal)** | **Standard 6-in lumber; S05-08 says 5–7 in** |
| Centre kingplank | 5½ in actual, 6-in nominal | Existing centre run; S05-08; standard stock |
| **Maximum plank length** | **12 ft hard maximum** | **Standard 12-ft lumber; approved** |
| Preferred minimum board length | 4 ft (design preference, not a user limit); short closure boards at openings reported | Reconstruction/QA |
| Beam-sided stock | sets 1–3: 4.75 in; sets 4–23: 5.5 in | S05-07 |
| **Half-beam coverage per plank end** | **sets 1–3: 2.375 in; sets 4–23: 2.75 in** | **Centreline butt: two boards share the beam** |
| Beam-centre tolerance | ±1/16 in in the model | Implementation/QA tolerance |
| Minimum internal butt clearance | 12 in from an opening coaming or the end of a continuous deck piece | Reconstruction; standard practice |
| Stagger | Adjacent runs ≥ two beam bays / 4 ft apart; never the same beam | Proposal |
| Waterway | Preliminary 5.5 in wide × 5 in deep oak; **to be measured** | S06-18/S07-16; S07-V02 trace, not printed |

The 12-ft limit is a **maximum**, not a target that must be reached. Actual
boards may be shorter; their lengths are set by the selected beam centres. The
QA check enforces `length <= 12 ft` and reports the min/max/mean. Boards under
4 ft are reported for review; short closure boards at openings are acceptable.

**Stock note:** the 4-in/6-in nominal widths have dressed actual widths of 3½
in and 5½ in. The 1.75-in deck thickness is taken from the drawing; it is not a
standard dressed 2-in thickness (1½ in), so either full-thickness stock or a
two-inch rough board is implied. The thickness is kept at the printed dimension
unless the user directs otherwise.

### 4.3 Pattern rules

- **Standard widths:** use 4-in nominal (3½ actual) for the typical field
  planks and 6-in nominal (5½ actual) for the centre kingplank and any inboard
  planks that are clearly wider in S05. Do not interpolate to 3.75/6.0. When a
  course is tapered at bow/stern, its width reduces by the spiling, but the
  stock it is cut from remains 4-in or 6-in nominal.
- **Beam-centred ends:** Every internal butt must be at the **centreline of a
  measured deck beam**. In plan, the upstream board ends at the beam centre,
  covering half the beam’s sided width; the downstream board begins at the same
  centreline, covering the other half. Both boards must bear on the beam. The
  joint is not offset to a beam edge, and no butt block or ledger substitutes
  for the beam.
- **Maximum length:** Work forward from the start of each continuous deck
  piece. When the remaining piece is longer than 12 ft, place a butt at a
  measured beam centre no more than 12 ft from the previous butt (or piece
  start) and no less than the preferred 4 ft. Never place a butt at a
  non-beam station. If no beam can satisfy both the 12-ft maximum and the
  stagger, report the conflict rather than adding a ledger or exceeding 12 ft.
- **Runs:** keep the existing centre + port/starboard course arrangement. Re-
  space the courses to standard 3½-in actual widths; the course boundaries are
  spiled/tapered, not constant-width parallel lines, so the outer runs narrow
  toward bow/stern and follow the waterway.
- **Stagger:** number the runs across the deck. Adjacent runs never share a
  beam; at least two bays (≥4 ft) separate adjacent butts. Use the beam grid
  to advance the phase by two bays per run; if the 12-ft cap and the stagger
  conflict in a short piece, prioritize the beam support and 12-ft cap, and
  report the reduced stagger.
- **Openings:** cut each plank individually at the coaming/header. Opening
  edges are framed boundaries, not internal butts; do not force a second header
  or a butt line across the deck at an opening. A plank that must stop at a
  coaming is supported by the existing header/carling.
- **Ends:** taper/spile the last 2–4 ft of each run into the waterway; do not
  leave a triangular tip narrower than the practical minimum plank width
  (target ≥ 2.5 in at the tip, or terminate the run one course earlier).
- **Kingplank:** implemented as a continuous kingplank within each continuous
  deck piece, in 6-in nominal (5½ actual) stock. It has no field-style butt
  joints; one visible 14-in scarf is cut in the longest piece.
- **Waterway:** the waterway is a separate oak member; deck plank ends butt to
  its inboard face and are fastened to it. The rail/covering board is separate.
  No generic backing slab is permitted.

### 4.4 Pattern generation algorithm (implementation sketch)

```
MAX_LEN = 12.0 ft
PREFERRED_MIN_LEN = 4.0 ft
deck = accepted deck envelope
waterway = separate oak margin member (section TBD)
field = deck minus waterway

runs = centre kingplank + port/starboard courses
       (standard 3.5-in / 5.5-in actual widths; boundaries spiled
        to the source waterway curve)

for run_index, run in enumerate(runs):
    pieces = continuous deck pieces between openings
    previous = butt stations of the adjacent run
    for piece in pieces:
        supported = measured beams whose member passes under the run
                    and whose centre lies inside the piece
                    (full/header/half beams; no split beam inside
                     the plank width)
        last = piece.start
        while piece.end - last > MAX_LEN:
            target = last + MAX_LEN
            candidates = supported beams with
                         last + PREFERRED_MIN_LEN <= centre <= target
                         and centre strictly inside the piece
            choose the candidate nearest target that
              - is >= 4 ft (two bays) from the adjacent run's butts
              - does not put two adjacent planks on the same beam
            if no candidate: report a support/stagger conflict;
                             do not invent a ledger or exceed 12 ft
            record the beam centre as a butt station
            last = beam centre
        # the final piece from last to piece.end is <= 12 ft
    split each run at the selected beam centres
    each internal board end = beam centreline; both boards share the beam
    trim/taper the run boundary at the waterway
register boards + waterway; no backing, no ledger
```

The split must use the **beam centreline**, not the beam edge. The two resulting
boards then have end faces at the centreline; their footprints each cover half
the beam’s sided width. The beam’s top face is below the deck, so both boards
sit on the same beam.

**Figure D note:** the current proposal figure plots the butt concept on the
*existing* run boundaries (3.75-in / 6.0-in) to show the beam-centred stations;
the implementation will re-space the courses to 3.5-in / 5.5-in actual
standard widths. The figure is a layout concept, not final board geometry.

### 4.5 Board identity and data

- Part names: `Deck/Planking/<run>/Board NN`.
- Per-board record: run, board number, forward start/end, actual length,
  actual width, nominal stock (`4-in` or `6-in`), material, start/end butt beam
  (set and centre station), observed/inferred status.
- Do not call an arbitrary opening-separated piece a “segment”; use “board”
  only when it is a physical plank with a recorded end condition.
- Keep the existing 109-piece conservation ledger as a migration baseline, but
  the new partition must replace it, not be layered on top.

### 4.6 Required checks

1. **Geometry:** every board is a valid single solid; no zero-area or sliver
   boards.
2. **Material:** `board volume + waterway volume == deck envelope volume`
   within the established 1-ppm high-accuracy guard.
3. **Standard stock:** every field board has an actual width of 3½ in (4-in
   nominal) or 5½ in (6-in nominal), except a documented tapered/spiled end or
   closure; no 3.75/6.0 interpolated widths remain.
4. **Maximum length:** no board exceeds 12 ft. Report min/max/mean and list any
   board under the 4-ft design preference.
5. **Beam-centred butt:** for every internal butt, the upstream board’s end and
   the downstream board’s start are both at the beam centreline within ±1/16
   in; both boards overlap the beam footprint; the beam is under the full plank
   width; each board covers half the beam’s sided width. No unsupported butt
   and no ledger.
6. **Stagger:** for every pair of adjacent runs, the internal butt centres are
   at least two beam bays / 4 ft apart; no adjacent planks share a beam; report
   the minimum stagger and any conflict.
7. **Clearances:** internal butts ≥ 12 in from opening coamings and the ends of
   continuous pieces; no board end blocks a hatch, stair, helm, centreboard
   trunk or mast partner.
8. **Ends:** no triangular deck tip narrower than the stated minimum; report
   the tip widths at bow/stern.
9. **Export:** every board has a mesh node and no face has zero triangles.
10. **Visual:** regenerate the plan, bow/stern details and a source overlay.

The checks are permanent: they run after every build and write the acceptance
record to `checks/m4_deck_pattern_acceptance.json`.

### 4.7 Side planking (eventual extension)

The same principles apply to the hull:

- Every internal butt at the **centreline of a measured frame**, with the two
  adjoining planks each covering half the frame’s sided width and sharing the
  frame. No ledger substitutes for a frame.
- Adjacent strakes staggered by at least two frame bays; no common frame line.
- Standard lumber: 12-ft maximum length and standard 4-in/6-in widths where the
  source allows.
- No butt at the chine, rabbet, sheer/waterway, stem or transom knuckle.
- Spile/taper strakes at bow and stern; no triangular tips.
- Keep physical-board IDs separate from waterline paint regions.
- Fix the existing `parts/planking.py` completeness defect first. The current
  prototype misses starboard courses 11 and 14; the one-shot split plus
  profile-position classification is the proposed replacement.

### 4.8 Migration / impact

- `parts/deck_planking.py` and `checks/prototype_deck_boards.py` are replaced
  or extended; `checks/check_registered_deck.py` and
  `drawing_package/m4_deck_partition.json` must be regenerated.
- Board count will change; export/build time and the part tree change.
- The course re-spacing from 3.75/6.0 to 3.5/5.5 actual changes the deck
  partition and the material conservation ledger; this is expected.
- The verified deck beams, stern envelope, mast and opening frames must not be
  altered by this work.
- The waterway is a separate increment; its section needs a labeled source
  detail before it is cut from the deck envelope.
- No new butt blocks/ledgers are added: the deck beams are the only support.

## 5. Decisions resolved

- **Length:** 12 ft is a hard maximum; shorter boards are allowed. Boards under
  4 ft are reported, not rejected; 31 exist as closures.
- **Widths:** standard 4-in nominal (3½-in actual) field stock and 6-in nominal
  (5½-in actual) inboard/kingplank stock.
- **Joint:** the two boards meet exactly at the beam centreline; each covers
  half the beam’s sided width. The ±1/16-in model tolerance is accepted. No
  fastening block or ledger is used.
- **Waterway:** the deck margin outboard of the clamp is separated provisionally
  in the live model. A measured section detail is still required before it is
  claimed final.
- **Kingplank:** continuous within each continuous deck piece; one visible scarf
  is modelled in the longest piece.
- **Opening edges:** the existing headers/carlings are the intended support.
  Butts that lie on those lines use the header/half-beam itself; no extra header
  or butt line is added.
- **Scope:** deck pattern first; the side pattern follows the same support and
  standard-lumber logic after the existing hull-board prototype defect is fixed.

## 6. Recommendation

The deck rules in §4.3 and the checks in §4.6 are implemented and passing. The
standard-lumber field, beam-centred shared butts, 12-ft cap and kingplank scarf
are in the live model; the deck beams are unchanged. The next increments are
the measured waterway section, spiled/tapered edge treatment and the
side-planking pattern. The build currently exceeds the 600-s target and needs
a separate performance pass before the side boards add more parts.
