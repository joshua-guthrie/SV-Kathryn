# M4 deck planking — gap analysis and proposed staggered pattern

**Status: review draft, 2026-11-11. No model geometry has been changed by this
analysis.** This document responds to the observation that the deck planks do
not all end together and that the current continuous-run partition would not be
as strong as a properly staggered, supported deck.

Figures are generated by:

```sh
python checks/build_deck_planking_gap_assets.py
```

They are written to `renders/` (not versioned); the script is versioned.

## 1. Summary

The current M4 deck is a **material partition**, not a plank schedule:

- 109 single-solid segments in 53 longitudinal runs (`parts/deck_planking.py`).
- Runs are parallel constant-width strips, split only where a hatch/cabin
  opening crosses them.
- There are no physical plank lengths, no staggered butt joints, and no
  supporting butt blocks/ledgers.
- At the bow and stern the strips are simply cut by the hull outline, leaving
  triangular tips rather than spiled/tapered plank ends.
- The deck boards currently include the material at the deck margin; the oak
  waterway is not a separate member.

Sheet 5 (S05-V01) shows longitudinal deck planks whose seams converge and
terminate on the curved waterway at **different fore-aft stations**, and the
openings interrupt individual planks. This is the staggered/tapered pattern the
model does not yet represent. The note does **not** print individual butt
positions or plank lengths, and the photos cannot resolve them; the butt layout
below is therefore a qualified reconstruction, not a measured schedule.

## 3. Gap analysis

| ID | Gap | Current model | Required / source evidence | Consequence | Proposed correction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | No physical plank lengths or butts | Each run is one continuous solid from opening to opening | Real planks are separate boards with ends; S05-08 implies timber stock, not 40-ft continuous material | Unrealistic construction; no joint strength or stock accounting | Split every run into individual boards on a supported butt grid |
| **G2** | Aligned ends at openings | Adjacent planks end on the same transverse lines at each opening | Sheet 5 cuts planks individually around openings; ends are staggered at the margin | A continuous weak transverse line; water ingress/fastener load concentration | Stagger opening-adjacent ends; use supported ledgers/butts; no common line across adjacent planks |
| **G3** | Parallel strips, triangular tips | Constant-width x-plane strips cut by the hull outline | Seams converge and plank ends taper/spile to the curved waterway | Weak short-grain tips; wrong plan appearance at bow/stern | Spile/taper course boundaries to the waterway; trim tips to a sensible plank width |
| **G4** | No butt support | No butts exist; nothing lands on beams | Strength requires a butt over a beam or a supported butt block/ledger | Any added butt would be unsupported in a span | Butt only over a measured beam or a 12-in block/ledger spanning two beams |
| **G5** | Waterway material conflated | Deck boards include the margin up to the hull outline | Oak waterway is a separate member; planks taper to it | Wrong material/edge detail; plank ends not housed correctly | Model the waterway separately (qualified section) and trim boards at its inboard face |
| **G6** | No board identity or metadata | Names are “segment NN” by run | Physical boards need IDs, length, butt support, material, inference status | Cannot validate stagger/support or report stock | Store per-board metadata in the partition ledger and part names |
| **G7** | No stagger/support regression check | Conservation/export only | Pattern must be verifiable after every build | A future edit could silently reintroduce aligned/unsupported joints | Add the checks in §4.6 |
| **G8** | Side planking has the same issue | Hull-board prototype exists but is not live | Same staggered/supported rules at frame stations | Same weakness on the hull; prototype also has an incomplete-course defect | Fix the prototype’s completeness first; then apply the pattern to the sides |

**Related prototype defect (not a deck-planking gap but part of the same work):**
`parts/planking.py` / `checks/prototype_hull_boards.py` currently fail on the
starboard side: the sequential boundary cuts miss courses **11 and 14**, and
the partition reports 17 cells with two tiny slivers instead of 16 complete
courses. The one-shot split + profile-classification approach is the proposed
fix to validate before any hull-board registration. Do not make the hull-board
prototype live until it passes.

## 4. Proposed deck planking specification

### 4.1 Goals / acceptance

1. Every deck plank is a separate valid single solid with a logical board ID.
2. Every butt joint is supported by a measured deck beam or a 12-in butt
   block/ledger spanning two beams; no unsupported butt exists.
3. Adjacent planks are staggered by at least two beam bays (approximately 4–6
   ft); no two adjacent planks share a butt station; no continuous transverse
   butt line crosses the deck.
4. Plank ends are spiled/tapered into the waterway; no triangular strip tips.
5. The total deck material still equals the accepted deck envelope
   (boards + waterway). Butt blocks/ledgers are additional material below the
   deck, not carved from the envelope.
6. Openings, stairs, helm clearances and hatch access remain clear.
7. The actual export has all board/butt-block meshes and no missing faces.

### 4.2 Parameters (all qualified)

| Parameter | Proposed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Deck plank thickness | 1.75 in | S05-08 |
| Typical plank width | 3.75 in (within printed 3.5–4 in) | S05-08 |
| Inboard plank width | 6 in (within printed 5–7 in) | S05-08; existing centre run |
| Centre kingplank | 6 in wide, continuous/scarfed on the same butt grid | Existing M4 partition; S05-08 |
| Target plank length | **16–20 ft** | Reconstruction, not printed; requires review |
| Butt block/ledger | 12 in long × plank width × 1.75 in thick, below deck | Reconstruction for support; material per local schedule |
| Minimum butt clearance | 12 in from opening coaming, plank end/waterway, mast/trunk | Reconstruction; standard practice |
| Stagger | Three-phase; adjacent planks ≥ two beam bays / 4 ft | Proposal |
| Waterway | Preliminary 5.5 in wide × 5 in deep oak; **to be measured** | S06-18/S07-16; S07-V02 trace, not printed |

The 16–20-ft length and 12-in block are deliberately marked as reconstruction.
If the user can mark specific butt stations on sheet 5, those observations
should replace the reconstructed grid.

### 4.3 Pattern rules

- **Runs:** keep the existing centre + port/starboard course arrangement. The
  course boundaries are spiled/tapered, not constant-width parallel lines, so
  the outer runs narrow toward bow/stern and follow the waterway.
- **Butt only on a support:** find the measured beams and opening headers; a
  butt may be centered on a full/header beam, or on a 12-in butt block/ledger
  that spans to the two nearest beams. Do not place a butt in the middle of a
  beam span without a ledger.
- **Stagger:** number the runs across the deck. Use a three-phase cycle over the
  supported stations (0, 2, 4 bays in a six-bay cycle). Adjacent runs never
  share a station; at least two bays separate adjacent butts. In sparse
  opening-limited zones, repeat the cycle every three runs rather than forcing
  aligned or unsupported joints.
- **Openings:** cut each plank individually at the coaming/header. Do not run a
  single transverse cut across all planks at an opening edge. A plank that must
  stop at a coaming gets a supported ledger/block; keep 12 in clear of the
  corner.
- **Ends:** taper/spile the last 2–4 ft of each run into the waterway; do not
  leave a triangular tip narrower than the practical minimum plank width
  (target ≥ 2.5 in at the tip, or terminate the run one course earlier).
- **Kingplank:** keep the 6-in centre run; use the same support grid with a
  different phase so its butts do not align with the adjacent port/starboard
  planks.
- **Fastening/edge:** the waterway is a separate oak member; deck plank ends
  butt to its inboard face and are fastened to it. The rail/covering board is
  separate. No generic “backing slab” is permitted.

### 4.4 Pattern generation algorithm (implementation sketch)

```
deck = accepted deck envelope
waterway = separate oak margin member (section TBD)
field = deck minus waterway

runs = centre kingplank + port/starboard courses
       (boundaries spiled to the source waterway curve)

supported = measured full/header beams + allowed ledgers
            (exclude split sets inside openings; exclude beams
             within 12 in of an opening coaming)

for run_index, run in enumerate(runs):
    targets = evenly spaced 16–20 ft butt targets along the run
    phase = three-phase offset from run_index
    for target in targets + phase:
        if a beam is within 1.5 ft: use that beam
        elif a supported ledger fits: use the ledger
        else: shift the target to the next supported station
        record butt(board, station, support, inferred=True)

split each run at its recorded butt stations and at opening edges
trim/taper the run boundary at the waterway
add the 12-in butt block/ledger under each butt
register boards + waterway + blocks; no backing
```

### 4.5 Board identity and data

- Part names: `Deck/Planking/<run>/Board NN`.
- Per-board record: run, board number, forward start/end, actual length,
  width, material, butt support (`beam set N` or `ledger`), observed/inferred
  status.
- Do not call an arbitrary opening-separated piece a “segment”; use “board”
  only when it is a physical plank with a recorded end condition.
- Keep the existing 109-piece conservation ledger as a migration baseline, but
  the new partition should replace it, not be layered on top.

### 4.6 Required checks

1. **Geometry:** every board/butt block is a valid single solid.
2. **Material:** `board volume + waterway volume == deck envelope volume`
   within the established 1-ppm high-accuracy guard. Butt blocks/ledgers are
   additional and reported separately.
3. **Support:** each butt’s block/beam overlap is real (not a bbox-only test).
4. **Stagger:** for every pair of adjacent runs, butt stations are at least two
   beam bays / 4 ft apart; no more than one of any two adjacent planks shares a
   station; report the minimum stagger.
5. **Clearances:** butts ≥ 12 in from opening coamings/ends; no butt blocks
   block hatches, stair, helm, centreboard trunk or mast partners.
6. **Ends:** no triangular deck tip narrower than the stated minimum; report
   the tip widths at bow/stern.
7. **Export:** every board/block has a mesh node and no face has zero triangles.
8. **Visual:** regenerate the plan, bow/stern details and a source overlay.

### 4.7 Side planking (eventual extension)

The same principles apply to the hull:

- Butts at measured frame stations or supported ledgers, never in a span.
- Adjacent strakes staggered by at least two frame bays; no common frame line.
- No butt at the chine, rabbet, sheer/waterway, stem or transom knuckle.
- Spile/taper strakes at bow and stern; no triangular tips.
- Keep physical-board IDs separate from waterline paint regions.
- Fix the existing `parts/planking.py` completeness defect first. The current
  prototype misses starboard courses 11 and 14; the one-shot split plus
  profile-position classification is the proposed replacement.

### 4.8 Migration / impact

- `parts/deck_planking.py` and `checks/prototype_deck_boards.py` are replaced
  or extended; `checks/check_registered_deck.py` and
  `drawing_package/m4_deck_partition.json` must be regenerated.
- Board count will change; export/build time and the part tree change.
- The verified deck beams, stern envelope, mast and opening frames must not be
  altered by this work.
- The waterway is a separate increment; its section needs a labeled source
  detail before it is cut from the deck envelope.

## 5. Open questions / decisions for review

1. **Plank lengths:** accept 16–20 ft as the reconstruction target, or can the
   actual butt stations be marked from sheet 5/photos?
2. **Butt support:** accept 12-in blocks/ledgers where no beam is available, or
   restrict all butts to measured beam centers?
3. **Waterway:** approve a measured section detail (width/depth/profile) before
   it is modeled; the preliminary 5.5 × 5 in is not final.
4. **Kingplank:** continuous with scarfs, or butted on the same stagger grid?
5. **Opening edges:** should the planks be individually cut to the coaming
   (proposed), or is a continuous header with a cover board preferred?
6. **Scope:** implement the deck pattern first, then the side pattern, or
   prototype both before replacing either live partition?

## 6. Recommendation

Approve the pattern rules in §4.3 and the support/check requirements in
§4.4–§4.6, with the 16–20-ft length and butt-block detail explicitly marked as
reconstruction. Then implement the deck pattern as a validated prototype
before replacing the live 109-piece partition. Keep the side-planking work
separate and fix the existing hull-board completeness defect first.

The current 659-part model, including the verified 23-set deck-beam schedule,
remains unchanged until that review is complete.
